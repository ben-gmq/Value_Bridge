## What a person can do when it lands
- In a project with the Waves component on, open **Waves** in the rail (under Control): add waves with a code, name,
  dates and status; drag them into order; retire and restore them.
- On a solution's page: split its effort across waves by percent (saved whole, exactly 100 % or empty) and say which
  solutions it depends on. A loop of dependencies is refused, naming the loop.
- Read the **wave plan**: effort per wave, plus what is in no wave yet (UNPHASED) and FRs with no solution
  (UNASSIGNED), adding up to the project's effort headline; warnings where a prerequisite sits in a later or the same
  wave, or in none.
- Freeze a baseline: the waves, splits and dependencies are in it, and a later baseline's comparison shows them.
- No screen, label, report or comparison area says "phase" (W-1).

## Grade (from spec §2)
- **Grade 1 — the hash-spec bump 5 → 6:** old baselines must keep verifying. Proof: freeze at spec 5, migrate, verify
  old and new; specs 2–5 stay pinned (`test_hash_spec_pins`); each migration's downgrade refuses once a spec-6 baseline
  or a row exists.
- **Grade 2 — the effort split and the no-cycle rule:** a wrong plan figure is something a client decides from.
  Criteria 1–3 through the API, with a rejected-but-split solution and an unrated FR in the identity test (R3). The
  cycle refusal is proven with a real concurrent pair on two connections, because the advisory lock is the whole
  guarantee.
- **Grade 3 — layout:** one browser pass.
- **Left uncovered on purpose:** wave status relabelling is covered by the existing code-list tests plus one
  PHASE_STATUS assertion, not a new suite.
